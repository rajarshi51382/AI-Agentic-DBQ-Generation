Essay Grading Rubric (0–7 Points)
1. Thesis / Claim (0–1 point)

1 point: Responds to the prompt with a clear, defensible thesis or claim that establishes a line of reasoning. The thesis must be more than a restatement of the prompt and should appear in the introduction or conclusion.

0 points: No defensible thesis is present.

2. Contextualization (0–1 point)

1 point: Describes a broader context relevant to the topic of the prompt (events, developments, or processes before, during, or after the time period). Goes beyond a passing phrase or reference.

0 points: No broader context provided or only vague mentions.

3. Use of Evidence (0–3 points)

1 point: Accurately describes evidence relevant to the prompt. (At least 3 specific pieces of evidence/examples, not just quotations or vague references.)

2 points: Supports an argument using at least 4 specific pieces of evidence. Evidence is tied to the argument, not just listed.

3 points: Effectively supports an argument using a wide range of specific evidence, including both examples tied to the prompt and evidence beyond the provided material (if applicable).

4. Analysis & Reasoning (0–2 points)

1 point: Explains how or why at least 2 pieces of evidence/examples are significant to the argument (goes beyond simple description). May address audience, purpose, point of view, or historical situation.

2 points: Demonstrates complex understanding of the topic by:

Exploring multiple perspectives, themes, or nuances.

Addressing similarities AND differences, or continuity AND change, or cause AND effect.

Connecting arguments across time periods or themes.

Effectively integrating evidence into a sophisticated argument.
